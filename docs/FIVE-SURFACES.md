# The five surfaces

Where each one stands, what it shares, and what is genuinely left.

Captures live in `docs/captures/`, one folder per surface, regenerated by
`node scripts/capture-surfaces.mjs` with the dev servers up.

## Status

| # | Surface | Ships as | Auth | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer | `apps/customer` — Expo, one binary per brand | anon / phone OTP | Built. On the demo data layer |
| 2 | Kiosk / POS | `apps/kiosk` — Expo, iPad landscape | device JWT | Built. Card tender simulated |
| 3 | Pickup display | `apps/display` — Next.js on a TV | device JWT, read-only | Built. Fixtures until a device pairs |
| 4 | Prep station | `apps/operator`, Prep tab | staff login | Built. Demo bake list |
| 5 | Crew | `apps/operator`, Crew tab | staff login | Built. Demo roster |

`apps/hq` is the sixth thing in `apps/` and is not one of the five: it is the
brand owner's console, not a surface in a shop.

## What they share

Everything below the screens is one copy, which is the point:

- **`@platform/domain`** — money, tax, order totals, sizes, fulfillment, the
  cart, menu options, loyalty rules, search, the row types. Framework-free, so
  `node:test` reaches all of it and the kiosk imports it without pulling in a
  UI.
- **`@platform/data`** — every read, under RLS. The display and the prep
  station consume the same functions, which is what stops them drifting.
- **`@platform/schema`** — the migrations and their TS mirrors, with a drift
  test that reads the SQL.
- **`@platform/ui`** — tokens and components. No screen hard-codes a colour,
  font, radius or spacing step.

One order state machine underneath all of it: `orders` holds current state,
`order_events` is append-only, and a BEFORE INSERT trigger is the only thing
that can move `orders.status`. Five surfaces can point at one row safely
because none of them writes status directly.

## What each surface does differently, and why

The design language is one language; what changes per surface is scale, density
and posture.

**Customer** — phone portrait, thumb-reachable. Money rides the primary action.

**Kiosk** — a *standing* guest, 2–3 feet away, with a queue behind them. 60pt
targets rather than 44, 20pt body rather than 16, the bag as a permanent rail
rather than a screen, and one linear task with no tab bar to wander into. An
idle clock resets an abandoned bag at 90s, but never counts down an untouched
attract screen — a kiosk nobody is using must not blank itself all day.

**Pickup display** — read at 15 feet, glanced at for under two seconds, running
unattended for fourteen hours. The ticket number is the largest thing on screen
by a wide margin. Almost nothing moves, because motion in peripheral vision
reads as an alarm; the sole exception is the curbside arrival badge, which is
the only urgent thing on an otherwise ambient board.

**Prep station** — a bench tablet read by someone with flour on their hands.
Two states and no more: the bake list, and one recipe. Scaled quantities keep
the recipe's own figure beside them, because a silently-rewritten number cannot
be checked against the card on the wall. The allergen banner is pinned and not
dismissible.

**Crew** — the one staff surface that is personal rather than device-paired,
because it shows names and attributes work to one. Un-ticking a checklist item
is allowed: a list people cannot correct is a list they stop trusting.

## The keystone: `devices`

Three of the five are screens nobody signs into. A `devices` table (migration
0022) mints a narrowly-scoped device JWT carrying no `role` claim at all, so a
device token can never satisfy a staff policy however it is crafted. Revocation
is a column, checked on every policy evaluation, so revoking in HQ takes effect
immediately rather than when a token expires.

| Role | May | May not |
|---|---|---|
| `display` | read one location's board, through a PII-narrow view | write anything |
| `kiosk` | read the menu, create an order | read other orders, read customers |
| `pos` | kiosk plus lookup and cash tender | refund, edit the menu |
| `prep` | read recipes, move its own batches | touch `orders` at all |

## What is genuinely left

Honest list, in rough order of what blocks the most:

1. **Nothing has touched a live backend.** No Supabase project migrated, no
   Square sandbox run. Every surface is on fixtures or the demo plane. The RLS
   policies and the state-machine trigger are written and unit-covered against
   the SQL, but not *exercised* — which is the difference between "written" and
   "known to work". `supabase start` plus the credentials in
   `docs/BUILD-REPORT.md` is the first step.
2. **The five-surface trace does not exist yet.** One order placed on the
   kiosk, appearing on the board, the display, the prep batch and the crew
   summary, asserted through the real `packages/data` queries. That test is the
   one that proves the surfaces are actually connected rather than merely
   consistent; it needs a live database.
3. **Card tender is simulated.** Square's Reader SDK needs a dev client, not
   Expo Go. `authorize()` in the kiosk's tender screen is the single seam.
4. **Menu imagery on the kiosk.** It reads the pure catalog and shows text
   tiles. `menu_items.image_url` exists; imagery should arrive with the rows
   rather than as a third copy of ~60 bundled files.
5. **Bundle identity.** `com.example.operator` still, and the kiosk and display
   need real ones before any build.
6. **The demo planes.** Customer and operator still run their own demo data;
   C1 points them at `placeOrder` and `menu_items`.

## Regenerating the captures

```bash
pnpm --filter @platform/display dev      # :3200
pnpm --filter @platform/operator web     # :4190 (or serve apps/operator/dist)
node scripts/capture-surfaces.mjs
```

A surface whose server is not running is skipped loudly rather than leaving an
empty folder, so a partial run says what it could not reach.
